The problem

What it's trying to solve

Anyone can write a product page that sells. The problem is how long it takes and how much rework it involves.

Take a dropshipper. The current workflow looks like this:

01

Copy the raw product spec from AliExpress / Temu / wherever.

02

Paste it into a chat tool.

03

Figure out how to make it actually good — the angle, the order, what to emphasize.

04

Paste it into the site.

05

It comes out flat, so go back and redo it. And again. And again.

That loop repeats for every single product. It's slow, repetitive, and the result is only as good as how well you happened to prompt it that time. Multiply it across a catalog and it becomes the real bottleneck.

How it works

One input, a full page out.

From the person's side it's a single step: paste a product URL. Behind that, Pagecraft does three things in sequence

01

Reads the product

It pulls and interprets the real product — what it is, who it's for, what makes it worth buying.

02

Decides the angle

It chooses a positioning and structure rather than dropping content into a fixed template.

03

Generates the page

It produces a full, conversion-ready landing page — hero, benefits, structure — in one pass.

From raw URL to page

What "decides the angle" actually means.

The difference between a template filler and Pagecraft is what happens between input and output. It interprets the product, decides the marketing and content angle, and proposes a layout and copy.

Input
A product URL — nothing more. No brief, no copy, no structure provided.
Output
A complete page with a chosen angle and structure
